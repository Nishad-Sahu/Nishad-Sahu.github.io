About

Research at the edge of autonomy, safety, and real-time systems.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in the Department of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Carnegie Mellon University. My work is centered on autonomous vehicles and intelligent transportation systems, with a particular interest in how safety-critical autonomous systems perceive, plan, and respond in difficult real road conditions.

At CMU, I am associated with CyLab and CADRE, and my research spans work-zone navigation, adverse-condition perception, co-simulation, cooperative route and behavior planning, and evaluation of end-to-end autonomous driving models.

Research

Research Themes

My current research asks how autonomous vehicles can operate more safely in environments where the road, weather, lighting, and surrounding behavior are changing quickly.

Autonomy

Safe AV operation in work zones

Studying how autonomous vehicles perceive, route, and plan behavior through complex roadway work zones and temporary traffic patterns.

Perception

Robust 3D perception

Developing and evaluating perception methods for lighting, weather, sensor, and distribution shifts that affect safety-critical driving systems.

Systems

Real-time embedded intelligence

Connecting real-time constraints, embedded systems, simulation, and cooperative planning into practical autonomous vehicle stacks.
